Energy calculation

Q = m × c × ΔT
1 × 4.186 × 50
Assumes constant specific heat, no phase change and no heat loss.

Interpretation

Water requires 209.3 kJ for this idealized temperature rise.

Real heating takes longer because the vessel, surroundings and heater have losses. The displayed time is an ideal lower-bound estimate.

Matter Database · Property comparison

Reference values, approximate near room temperature

Specific heat capacity comparison

Higher specific heat means more energy is needed to raise one kilogram by one kelvin. It does not mean the material is a fuel or contains more chemical energy.

Values vary with temperature, pressure, composition and phase. Air value is approximate at ordinary conditions. This initial database is illustrative and should be verified against a trusted property reference before formal research.

Important distinction

Specific heat: energy needed to change temperature.

Energy density: energy available per mass or volume, with a defined process or storage method.

Reaction enthalpy: heat absorbed or released by a specified chemical reaction.

Research warning

There is no universal “substance that supplies the most energy.” The answer depends on whether we mean fuel energy per kilogram, energy per litre, heat released in a particular reaction, or heat stored per kilogram over a temperature range.

Research Method · Experimental discipline

Protocol 01

How to run a valid experiment

  1. Write a precise question and hypothesis.
  2. Define the system boundary and what counts as input/output.
  3. Record material identity, purity, mass and starting conditions.
  4. Measure temperature, time, electrical input and surroundings.
  5. Repeat trials and report uncertainty.
  6. Compare measured values with model predictions.

Safety and evidence

This prototype does not provide instructions for energetic chemical reactions. Begin with simulations and low-voltage, supervised heating experiments. Do not mix unknown chemicals, heat sealed containers, or test fuels or reactive substances in an improvised setup.
