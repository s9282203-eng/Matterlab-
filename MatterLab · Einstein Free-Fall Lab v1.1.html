Einstein and free fall

The equivalence principle says that locally, free fall is indistinguishable from weightlessness in a small freely falling laboratory. In a small region, at speeds far below light and in a weak field, Newtonian constant-acceleration motion is an excellent approximation. The mechanics here is classical; only the time-dilation tab uses a relativistic formula.

Physical drop tests need a secured release, a clear drop zone and supervision. This simulation ignores wind, buoyancy, tumbling and altitude-dependent gravity.

Ideal free fall

y(t) = h − v₀t − ½gt²
v(t) = v₀ + gt
t_impact = (√(v₀² + 2gh) − v₀) / g
KE = ½mv²

Quadratic drag and terminal speed

m·dv/dt = mg − ½ρCdA·v|v|
v_t = √(2mg / (ρCdA))
τ = v_t / g (approach time scale)

At v_t drag balances weight and acceleration is zero. A fall shorter than a few τ never gets close to v_t.

Gravitational time dilation

r_s = 2GM / c²
dτ/dt = √(1 − r_s/r − u²/c²)
weak field: Δf ≈ g·Δh / c²

Differences are computed as exact algebraic differences, so tiny effects such as 10⁻¹⁵ keep their precision.
