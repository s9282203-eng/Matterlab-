Six physics areas

1 · ThermodynamicsGas temperature and available thermal energy underpin the energy available for propulsion.
2 · Gas dynamics & fluid mechanicsExpanding gas flow, pressure changes and momentum transfer produce thrust.
3 · Atmospheric physicsAir density decreases with altitude, changing aerodynamic resistance.
4 · Newtonian mechanicsNet force controls acceleration through powered ascent and coast.
5 · Energy & momentum conservationPropulsive momentum creates upward speed; kinetic energy converts to gravitational potential energy.
6 · Aerodynamics & gravityDrag opposes motion while gravity slows the rocket until its vertical speed reaches zero.

How the model works

Every 2 ms step solves Newton’s second law with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta integrator:

a = [ T − ½·ρ(y)·Cd·A·(v−w)|v−w| ] / m(t) − g·(R/(R+y))²
  • ρ(y) = ρ₀·e−y/H: thinner air aloft means less drag.
  • m(t) falls linearly to burnout mass, so acceleration rises as the rocket lightens.
  • Ideal Δv = Vₑ·ln(m₀/m_f) is what the motor adds in free space; the shortfall to burnout speed is the drag + gravity loss.
  • The integration is split exactly at burnout, and the apex is interpolated where v crosses zero.
